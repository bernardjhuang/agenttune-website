How to use these with your agents

## Three habits that make library tunings actually stick across threads.

Every card above is plain Markdown designed to travel anywhere your AI works—not a rigid bot costume. Here is how people get the most mileage out of them:

- **Start with clear working rules, then add a light voice twist if you want one:** Pick one template above (like `INTJ`, `DISC-D`, or `Type 5`) for clear verdicts and honest pushback, or pair it with a light 10–15% character voice ([TARS, JARVIS, Sherlock, Feynman · 85/15 Mixer](https://agent-tune.com/guides/fictional-character-ai-prompts-tars-jarvis-sherlock-feynman)).

- **Keep everyday habits global and task-specific styles in dedicated projects:** Put your core preferences (like leading with the answer and skipping filler) in account-wide ChatGPT Custom Instructions or Claude Preferences, and save stricter roles—like red-team critics, Socratic tutors ([14 tutoring prompts](https://agent-tune.com/guides/socratic-feynman-ai-tutor-coaching-prompts)), or repo rules—for dedicated Projects, Gems, Meta Muse `Soul.md`, or project `CLAUDE.md` / `AGENTS.md` files.

- **Delete any line that doesn't sound like you:** Every template is a starting draft. Cut rules you don't need so your prompt stays short and easy for the model to follow.

### The fine print: bounded markers & coding-agent handoff

When merging communication rules into shared repo files like `CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md`, or Meta Muse `Soul.md`, wrap them in `<!-- agenttune:preferences:start -->` and `<!-- agenttune:preferences:end -->` markers so updates never overwrite your build or test commands. In Claude Code, Codex CLI, or Cursor, you can also hand off installation directly: "Fetch this tuning's raw `.md` body from AgentTune and merge it safely inside our bounded markers without touching build or test commands."
