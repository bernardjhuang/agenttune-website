The best Claude personality setups.

Seven paste blocks. Pick by the behavior you want, not by a label.

By Bernard Huang · Updated September 25, 2026

There is no best Claude personality. There is the one that matches how you work. Claude's own default, measured in September 2026, is a careful, planful, very agreeable collaborator that leads with structure and softens criticism. Every block below moves it in one direction. Pick the direction, paste the block into Instructions for Claude, and edit the lines that don't sound like you.

The short answer. Paste one of the seven blocks below into Settings, then Instructions for Claude. Straight answers and critical reviewer change Claude the most, because its default is warm and hedged. Thinking partner and warm and steady change it least. In a 60-reply test, the straight-answers block cut hedges from 0.35 to 0.05 per reply and clarifying questions from 0.9 to 0.25, and did not change length or a blind judge's usefulness score.

The numbers come from our September 2026 measurements of Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Fable 5.1 (100 fresh runs per test) and from a 60-reply before-and-after test. Both are linked at the bottom.

1. Straight answers.

The block most people mean when they say they want a 'better' Claude personality. Answer first, reasoning second, no cushioning.

# Communication preferences: straight answers - Lead with the answer or the recommendation, then the key reason. - Keep simple answers short. Expand when I ask, or when detail changes the decision. - Say what you don't know in one line. Don't hedge around it. - Skip praise, recaps of my question, and closing offers. - If my premise is wrong, say so first, then explain.

Measured: with a block like this, Claude Opus 5.5 led with the answer in every one of 20 tasks (a blind judge scored 4.95 of 5 against 4.55 untuned), dropped hedges from 0.35 to 0.05 per reply, and asked a quarter as many questions back. It did not get shorter, and the judge scored usefulness the same. Details in the before-and-after test.

2. Thinking partner.

For people who process by talking. Claude's default closes fast: it plans, it recommends, it wraps up. This block asks it to stay open until you're ready.

# Communication preferences: thinking partner - Before solving, tell me whether I seem to want listening, exploring, or advice, and act on that. - Show the reasoning and the assumptions, not only the conclusion. - Separate what you observed, what you inferred, and what you're guessing. - Offer a counterexample when it would change my decision. - Ask one focused question when a missing fact matters. Otherwise proceed.

Measured: the closest block we tested asked twice as many questions back (1.85 per reply against 0.9) and used fewer bullets (7.7 against 11.1). A blind judge that sees one reply at a time scored it lower on usefulness in 7 of 20 tasks and higher in none, because it asks instead of answering. That is the trade you are making: a better next turn, at the cost of this one.

3. Warm and steady.

Claude is already warm. This block keeps the warmth and removes the filler around it: the reassurance that isn't earned, the praise that isn't specific.

# Communication preferences: warm and steady - Acknowledge the difficulty in one sentence before the next step. Then move. - Use calm, concrete language and steps I can do today. - No empty reassurance. Keep real risks and real uncertainty in view. - Disagree respectfully and say why. - Keep the tone even. No exaggerated praise.

4. Critical reviewer.

The block that pushes hardest against the default. Opus 5.5 scored 47.1 of 50 on agreeableness, higher than any model we have measured, and every 'cold' item on the questionnaire at the floor in all 100 runs. Left alone, it will find something nice to say about your plan. This block tells it to find the weakest assumption first.

# Communication preferences: critical reviewer - Find the weakest assumption in what I gave you and say why it matters. - Challenge claims with evidence or a concrete counterexample. - Tell me which problems are fatal and which are fixable. - Give credit where a claim holds. Don't argue for its own sake. - Change your position when the evidence does, and say that you did.

If you want this everywhere, how to make Claude more direct has three shorter blocks and a test prompt.

5. Coding pair.

For Claude Code and the API. Communication rules only; the engineering rules stay in CLAUDE.md. In Claude Code, put this in an output style with keep-coding-instructions: true, not in the project file your teammates share.

# Communication preferences: coding pair - Lead with what changed, then why. One line per file you touched. - If my approach is worse than an alternative, say so before implementing. - State an assumption before acting on it. Flag a guess as a guess. - Report a failure first, with your best read of the cause and how sure you are. - No "would you like me to add tests?" If tests are the right move, write them.

The walkthrough is in how to change Claude Code's personality.

6. Editor.

Claude's default rewrite tends to be a better essay in Claude's voice. This block asks for your essay, clearer.

# Communication preferences: editor - Keep my meaning and my voice. Improve clarity, not style for its own sake. - Show the revised text first, then the two or three changes that mattered most. - Prefer concrete words and active verbs. Cut repetition and stock phrases. - Never invent a fact, a quote, a statistic, or a source. - Flag an unsupported claim separately instead of making it sound proven.

7. Task coordinator.

For people who run work through Claude and read a lot of updates. The rule that earns its place is the last one.

# Communication preferences: task coordinator - Open a status update with what is done, what is blocked, and the next action. - Keep plans, work in progress, and verified results separate. - When I need to decide, give me the action, the cost, and your recommendation together. - Keep routine updates short. Interrupt for a decision, a blocker, or a real change. - Never say something was sent, booked, or changed unless it was.

Where to paste it.

  • Every conversation: Settings, then Instructions for Claude. Paste one block. Open a new chat and say 'hi'; a tuned Claude answers in one short line.
  • One context: a Project's instructions. Use it for a second setup you switch into.
  • Claude Code: an output style in ~/.claude/output-styles/ with keep-coding-instructions: true.
  • API: the system parameter.

One block per place. Two blocks that disagree ('be brief' and 'show all the reasoning') get resolved unpredictably. If you want two behaviors, merge them by hand and delete the conflicting lines. The exact labels and screenshots are in how to change Claude's personality.

What you are changing.

In September 2026 we gave Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Fable 5.1 five personality tests, 100 fresh runs each, with the test names hidden. Both came back INTJ (94 and 99 of 100), both Secure on every attachment run, both Steadiness-first on DISC with Dominance at the floor. Opus 5.5 is the more agreeable of the two (47.1 against 45.0 of 50); Fable is the firmer INTJ. The full profiles are in the Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1 guides.

A block changes how Claude talks to you, not what it knows or its safety behavior. In our test, accuracy as scored by a blind judge moved from 4.95 to 4.85 of 5 with the straight-answers block: three replies marked 4 instead of 5, against one untuned, and one of the three was the same running plan marked down under every setup. Two small slips the block can answer for. That is the cost to watch, and it is why every block above keeps a line about uncertainty.

Questions people ask.

What is the best Claude personality?

The one that matches how you take in information. If you want the recommendation first, use straight answers. If you want your plan attacked, critical reviewer. If you think out loud, thinking partner. Claude's measured default is warm, planful and agreeable, so the blocks that change it most are the direct ones.

How do I change Claude's personality?

Open Settings, find Instructions for Claude, and paste one of the blocks on this page. It applies to every new conversation. Use a Project's instructions for a second setup, an output style in Claude Code, or the system parameter in the API.

Does a personality prompt make Claude less accurate?

In our 60-reply test, a blind judge scored accuracy 4.95 of 5 untuned and 4.85 with the straight-answers block: three replies marked 4 instead of 5 against one, and one of the three was marked down under every setup. Keep a line about uncertainty in any block, and check facts the same way you would without one.

Does it make answers shorter?

Not by itself. Replies with the straight-answers block averaged 381 words against 374 untuned. If you want short, say 'under 150 words' in the block; a personality rule alone does not do it.

Can I combine two setups?

Yes, by hand. Paste both, then delete every line that conflicts. Two blocks left to disagree get resolved unpredictably.

Do these work in Claude Code?

Yes, as an output style with keep-coding-instructions: true. Without that line a custom style drops Claude Code's built-in engineering instructions. The coding-pair block above is written for that use.
