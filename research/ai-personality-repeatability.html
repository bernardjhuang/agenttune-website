AI personality repeatability: does a stable label mean stable answers?

By Bernard Huang · Updated November 17, 2026 · ~3 min read · Markdown (.md) · MCP · Briefs JSON

A model can get the exact same personality label 100 times in a row while changing dozens of individual answers underneath.

Summary: All 400 fresh Attachment runs scored Secure, yet the four models produced 90, 100, 79, and 96 different answer sheets out of 100. A steady label often hides shifting individual answers.

Each cell shows how many unique answer sheets appeared across 100 fresh runs. Shorter tests like DISC (16 questions) naturally repeat exact answer sheets more often than longer tests like the Big Five (50 questions).

On DISC, Opus 5.5 gave the exact same 16 answers 60 times

Across 100 DISC runs, Claude Opus 5.5 produced only 7 distinct answer sheets—and its single most common answer sheet appeared 60 times. Claude Fable 5.1 produced 10 distinct DISC sheets (top sheet: 45 times), while GPT-6 Sol produced 66 distinct sheets (top sheet: 8 times).

Remember that Opus and Fable took one hidden-name test per session, while Astra and Sol took all five named tests inside one Codex session. Both prompt style and sampling settings affect how much answers vary.

100% Secure labels, but up to 100 different answer sheets

Every single run across all four models (400/400) landed in the Secure Attachment quadrant. Yet GPT-6 Sol gave a different set of 36 answers on all 100 runs, Fable gave 96 unique sheets, Astra gave 90, and Opus gave 79. Any anxiety and avoidance averages below 4.00 get bucketed into Secure.

On MBTI, keeping tied axes open shows 98 resolved INTJ runs for Fable vs. 45 resolved ISTJ and 19 resolved INTJ runs for Astra (see our tie-breaking study).

Why averages need standard deviations beside them

GPT-6 Sol’s Neuroticism score averaged 20.05 out of 50 with a standard deviation of 5.35 points from run to run. Its Agreeableness score averaged 44.99 with a standard deviation of just 1.01 points. Looking only at the average hides which traits bounce around between sessions.

The fine print: how to test consistency on real work and download the data

Survey repeatability doesn't prove a model will follow your instructions on real tasks. In our November 2026 10,000-sheet expansion (Crystalline vs. Fluid AI Minds: 27.7× Persona Entropy Divide), per-item Shannon entropy spanned from 0.054 bits on Claude Fable 5.2 (only 2/100 unique DISC vectors and 8/100 unique Big Five vectors) up to 1.499 bits on DeepSeek V4 Pro, while disproving late-survey fatigue collapse (0.519 → 0.585 bits across items 1–50). To test real consistency, run the same task prompt 5–10 times in fresh chats and check observable rules: Did it put the conclusion first? Did it challenge a flawed assumption? Did it stay under your word limit? Download the 20-row repeatability CSV to inspect all counts from this study.

Questions people ask.

Does a 100% repeatable personality label mean an AI gives identical quiz answers every run?

No. Across 400 fresh Attachment runs, all four models tested as Secure 100% of the time, yet they produced 79 to 100 distinct 36-item answer sheets out of 100 runs because any anxiety and avoidance averages below 4.00 fall into the same Secure quadrant.

Which questionnaire showed the highest vs. lowest answer-sheet repeatability?

The 16-item DISC test had the fewest unique answer sheets (Claude Opus 5.5 produced only 7 distinct sheets across 100 runs, repeating its top sheet 60 times), whereas the 50-item Big Five and 36-item Attachment tests produced 79 to 100 unique answer sheets per 100 runs.
