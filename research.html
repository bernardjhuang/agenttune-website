Methods and limits.

The combined total of roughly 2,200 is a count of reported scoring records across five instruments, not 2,200 independent model responses. MBTI covers six models; the other reports cover four each.

  • Mixed MBTI protocols. The source report describes separate Claude sub-agent contexts, a Gemini automation loop, and GPT iterations against a scoring key. GLM, Grok, and MiniMax each supplied one self-assessment that was scored repeatedly. The 597/600 aggregate is therefore not an independent-response rate.
  • Unresolved source inconsistency. The source describes re-scoring one GLM vector but reports two different resulting types. Without the raw records and scorer version, we cannot reconcile that discrepancy. The counts below are attributed reports, not independently reproduced results.
  • Human questionnaires, model-generated answers. These instruments were not validated here as measures of AI personality. System prompts, agent overlays, item wording, sampling, and scorer choices can all affect a label. Cross-model differences cannot be attributed to training alone.
  • Simulation is separate. The Opus 4.8 follow-up sampled synthetic answers from one elicited probability profile. It is not 100 fresh model responses and cannot establish a change from Opus 4.7 under a different protocol.
  • Evidence still needed. A reproducible comparison needs versioned prompts, model settings, raw response records, scorer code and tie policy, plus matched protocols. No controlled evaluation of AgentTune versus generic instructions is published here. Example conversations are illustrative; no percentage improvement is established.
