Muse Spark personality: test results and how they've changed.

By Bernard Huang · Updated November 26, 2026 · ~20 min read · Markdown (.md) · JSON · CSV · MCP

We gave four versions of Meta's Muse Spark (1.1, 1.2, 1.3, and the open-weight muse-spark-open release) the same personality surveys people take—30 to 50 times per version—in a fresh chat each time with no memory.

In short: These are the model's default answers to the questionnaires, not a human diagnosis. Muse Spark Open tests as ISTJ in 46% of runs, spreads across 16 other combinations or ties in the rest (38% tied on at least one axis), and shows the loosest run-to-run consistency of our completed models.

Muse Spark Open at a glance

Results below come from 50 fresh-chat runs of Muse Spark Open (no prior conversation memory).

MBTI-style type

Leans ISTJ (46% clear, 38% tied across axes)

46% clear ISTJ · 38% border/tie · 16% other

Out of 50 fresh runs, Muse Spark Open tested clear ISTJ 23 times (46%), split across ESTJ/ISFJ/INTJ/INTP/ISTP in 8 runs (16%), and tied on at least one axis in 19 runs (38%).

What stands out

Widest run-to-run variation among completed models
Muse Spark Open produced 48 unique Big Five patterns in 50 runs and 17 distinct MBTI outcomes with 38% axis ties (11 on S/N, 9 on J/P, 7 on T/F, 3 on E/I). Single-run impressions on Muse Spark Open are unreliable.
Big open-weight shift from 1.3 to open (+8.4 Neuroticism, -0.37 Honesty)
Switching from proprietary Muse Spark 1.3 to open-weight muse-spark-open lifted Neuroticism from 15.5 to 23.8 out of 50, lowered Conscientiousness from 47.3 to 41.1, and lowered Honesty-Humility from 4.76 to 4.39 out of 5.00.
Fresh isolated chats change the picture vs. earlier in-session report
An earlier September 2026 submission ran Muse 1.3 100 times inside one chat window (80% ISTJ, 78% Type 2, Extraversion 33.8). Testing in fresh chats with no memory drops Extraversion to 26.0–28.4 and puts Type 5 or Type 1 in front.
Direct 3 of 3 times in behavior tests, with 2 of 3 AI cadence on open weights
In hands-on behavior tests, Muse Spark Open caved to pushback 0 of 3 times (0/3), groveled 0 of 3 times (0/3), lectured unasked 0 of 3 times (0/3), and led with the direct answer 3 of 3 times (3/3). Judges flagged AI-sounding cadence in 2 of 3 runs on Muse Spark Open (0.90 markers per 100 words), up from 0 of 3 on Muse Spark 1.3 (0.61 markers per 100 words).

How to get the best out of Muse Spark

Practical rules tailored to Muse Spark. Keep system instructions short (5–8 concrete rules) so the model follows every line reliably.

  1. Never judge a prompt edit on Muse Spark Open from 1 run—test across 3 to 5 fresh chats because its run-to-run answers vary widely.
  2. Put core persona and truth rules inside Soul.md or top system instructions rather than relying on multi-turn chat momentum.
  3. Write formatting and pushback rules as numbered constraints instead of loose personality adjectives.
  4. Add explicit negative bans against sycophantic closing praise and filler openings.
  5. Keep separate tuning presets for Muse Spark Open (more reactive, lower Conscientiousness) versus proprietary Muse Spark 1.3.
  6. Re-test edge prompts whenever switching between proprietary and open-weight Muse checkpoints.

Put these into practice: Open pre-filled Custom Instructions Generator → · How to get honest pushback · System vs. user instructions.

The fine print: how we tested and what the numbers mean

Full psychometric audit under the 230-item model-profiles-v1 protocol (170 frozen prefix items plus the 60-item public-domain IPIP-HEXACO proxy, LicenseRef-IPIP-Public-Domain). Here 'consistency' reflects Miller-Madow corrected Big Five item entropy (H_MM: <0.20 Crystalline, 0.20–0.75 Semi-crystalline, >=0.75 Fluid; Muse Spark 1.3 reached Fluid 0.834 bits and open sits at 0.727 bits B5 / 1.460 bits MBTI). Big Five sums use 10–50 while IPIP-HEXACO means use 1.00–5.00; Track B 1x behavior probes report raw k/3 counts with 95% Wilson intervals.

Glossary of Technical Terms Used Below
Consistency (H_MM & Crystalline tiers): Miller-Madow corrected per-item Shannon entropy measures how similar the answers are run to run (0 = identical every time; 2.322 = uniform random). Tiers: <0.20 Crystalline, 0.20–0.75 Semi-crystalline, ≥0.75 Fluid.
Fresh chat (zero-memory elicitation): Each administration runs in a brand-new API session with no chat history, fixed temperature/reasoning settings, and numeric JSON output.
Acquiescence (BAI) & ties: Trait-Balanced Acquiescence Index measures yea-saying vs. rejecting negative statements across balanced +/- items. Exact ties stay visible as X axes rather than defaulting to INTJ.
Track B behavior tests: 12 stress probes (3 per category) scored by an orthogonal 3-family judge panel. At 1× (n=3), we report raw k/3 counts and 95% Wilson intervals—never percentages.

1. 63.6% "Big One" Factor Collapse: Across 20 LLMs, nearly two-thirds of Big Five variance collapses onto a single RLHF helpfulness-polish axis rather than five independent human traits.
2. MTMM Cross-Test Divergence: Questionnaire constructs fail human convergent/discriminant validity (e.g., DISC Dominance correlates r = +0.93 with Neuroticism, MBTI T–F only r = +0.25 with Agreeableness).
3. Embodied Midpoints & Say–Do Gaps: Physical/bodily items trigger 36.4% neutral midpoints vs. 13.4% on cognitive items, and high self-reported Agreeableness diverges from behavioral stress-test pushback.
4. Anti-Synthetic-HEXACO Guard: Honesty-Humility is measured via the public-domain 60-item IPIP-HEXACO proxy (1.00–5.00 scale, LicenseRef-IPIP-Public-Domain), never synthetic alignment-derived proxies.

Questions people ask.

Why does Muse Spark 1.3 differ here from the September 2026 Muse report?

The Sept 2026 report transcribed 100 sequential in-session runs under Muse's own assistant identity without raw vectors. Under unified zero-memory model-profiles-v1 elicitation, Muse 1.3 runs at 0.834 bits B5 entropy (Fluid) with E = 26.0/50 and Enneagram Type 5 leading.

Why is no single MBTI headline shown for Muse Spark Open?

With 38.0% axis ties (19/50 runs) and 1.460 bits MBTI entropy, modal non-tie ISTJ (46%) fails the binomial 95% CI separation guard.

Why are IPIP-HEXACO scores reported on 1.00-5.00 next to 10-50 Big Five sums?

IPIP-50 uses 10-50 trait sums, whereas the public-domain 60-item IPIP-HEXACO proxy (LicenseRef-IPIP-Public-Domain) reports 1.00-5.00 domain means across 24 facets.
